# Children: safeguarding work required before inclusion

Status: **children are excluded from the pilot.** Children remain within Heartbeat's wider mission,
but the adult design in this repository is not suitable for them. This page lists the separate
work needed before any version of Heartbeat includes anyone under 18.

**Interim rule.** No one under 18 is enrolled. If a minor asks, staff follow the host's existing
youth and safeguarding procedures; Heartbeat records nothing about them. Abuse or neglect of a child
that staff learn about — from anyone — is reported to police and county child protective services
under OMH's [required-reports policy](safeguarding-reports.md).

## Why the adult design does not transfer

| Adult design assumption | Why it fails for children |
|---|---|
| The participant consents and controls the plan | Minors generally cannot give legally effective consent alone; a parent or guardian usually holds that authority. |
| Nobody else has a right to the record | Parents and guardians may have legal rights to information — and the person seeking access may be the parent or guardian who is causing harm. |
| "Neither confirm nor deny" to callers | Conflicts with a custodial parent's rights and with schools' and agencies' duties. |
| Withdrawal is honored without question | A child may be pressured to withdraw; a guardian may withdraw a child to stop follow-up. |
| Staff follow the host's ordinary procedures | North Carolina requires **any person** who suspects a juvenile is abused, neglected or dependent to report to county social services (G.S. 7B-301), and **any adult** who knows or should reasonably know a juvenile has been the victim of certain violent or sexual offenses or misdemeanor child abuse to report to **local law enforcement** (G.S. 14-318.6). Follow-up that uncovers harm will often create a legal duty to report, including to police ([legal-review.md](legal-review.md#2-reporting-duties-involving-juveniles--blocking)). |
| Contact sources are service organizations | For children the main contact is school, which brings federal student-records law (FERPA, 20 U.S.C. 1232g) and school districts' homeless-education liaisons under the McKinney-Vento Act (42 U.S.C. 11432) — not reviewed here; part of the legal analysis below. |
| Staff are trained for adult outreach | Youth work needs safeguarding vetting (background checks, two-adult rules) and youth-specific skills. |

## Work required first

1. **Legal analysis** (North Carolina counsel): consent and assent for minors, including
   unaccompanied and runaway youth and emancipated minors; parental and guardian rights to records;
   how 7B-301 and 14-318.6 reporting would work, and how that sits with Heartbeat's boundary against
   law-enforcement integrations (the duty belongs to the person, not the software, but the design
   must not obscure it); FERPA if schools are involved; federal Runaway and Homeless Youth program
   rules if the host receives those funds.
2. **A youth-specific consent and assent model**, developmentally appropriate, tested with young
   people with lived experience.
3. **A safeguarding policy** for the host and OMH: staff and volunteer vetting, supervision,
   two-adult rules, handling disclosures, and escalation to designated safeguarding leads.
4. **A partner** experienced in serving homeless and runaway youth, not an adult-serving
   organization stretching its remit.
5. **A design for the guardian problem**: when a guardian may see, change or end enrollment, and how
   to protect a child from a harmful guardian within the law.
6. **Turning 18**: what happens to a youth record at majority (re-consent as an adult, or deletion).
7. **Families**: whether a parent enrolled with children counts the children, and how — including
   homemakers whose isolation also affects their children.
8. **Its own pilot**, gates and evaluation, separate from the adult pilot.

Until this work is done and reviewed, the adult product must not be used for children, even
informally.
