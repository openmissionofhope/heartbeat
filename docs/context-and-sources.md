## Current practice

How homeless-response systems handle people who stop being seen:

* **Built for Zero (Community Solutions).** Communities must have "a written policy that specifies
  the number of days of inactivity (i.e. the person cannot be located) after which a person's status
  will be changed to 'inactive,' and which includes protocols to attempt to locate an individual
  before they are moved to inactive status", naming the responsible provider and "the number,
  method and frequency" of attempts. **No number of days is prescribed**; 90 days appears only for
  institutional stays. — *All Single Adults Quality Data Scorecard Rubric v1.0*, April 2024,
  https://community.solutions/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/All-Single-Adults-Quality-Data-Scorecard-Rubric-2024-Guidance-Document.pdf
* **VA Homeless Programs.** Inactivity is "typically 60-90 days, without contact or service
  engagement, despite diligent outreach efforts"; the stated purpose is that it "improves data
  quality". — *Inactive Procedure Guidance*, updated 2025-10-28,
  https://department.va.gov/homeless/wp-content/uploads/sites/72/2026/01/Inactive_Procedure_Guidance_ud2025-10-28.pdf
* **HUD HMIS.** Street-outreach clients "may be exited when the outreach staff has been unable to
  locate the client for an extended period of time", with the period set locally; automatic exits
  after a set absence are optional, and the destination "No exit interview completed" is treated as
  missing data. — *FY 2026 HMIS Data Standards Manual* (effective 2025-10-01),
  https://files.hudexchange.info/resources/documents/HMIS-Data-Standards.pdf
* **Asheville.** Records of the CoC's Emergency Shelter Development Work Group (2025–2026) refer to a
  by-name list, an unsheltered list and an inactive list. — PDF hosted by Blue Ridge Public Radio,
  https://npr.brightspotcdn.com/8d/fe/c2b9567545b09fa46fe71e4309fc/emergency-shelter-development-work-group-running-meeting-records.pdf

**What this supports.** Existing systems do try to locate people, but for list accuracy, over
locally set windows (often 60–90 days) not tied to the person's own expected contact, ending in a
status change or a missing-data exit rather than a welfare follow-up.

### Telephone reassurance programs (contrast)

Check-in programs for older adults commonly escalate a missed check-in to a designated contact and
then to a welfare check, sometimes by police: Maryland's Senior Call Check contacts an alternate and
then "non-emergency service providers" and "will not call 911"
(https://aging.maryland.gov/Pages/senior-call-check.aspx); Anne Arundel County asks police to perform
a wellness check (https://aacounty.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/telephone-reassurance-participant.pdf);
the Dutchess County Sheriff's "Are You OK?" program may "send an officer"
(https://www.dutchessny.gov/Departments/Sheriff/Sheriffs-Office-Are-You-OK-Program.htm). Heartbeat
differs deliberately: social-service responders only, follow-up limited to what the participant chose.

## Phones

| Study | Sample | Findings |
|---|---|---|
| Rhoades et al., "No Digital Divide? Technology Use among Homeless Adults", *J Soc Distress Homeless* 26(1), 2017. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31097900/ | 421 homeless adults entering permanent supportive housing, Los Angeles | 94% owned a cell phone, ~59% a smartphone; in the past 3 months 56% changed phones and 55% changed numbers |
| Raven et al., "Mobile Phone, Computer, and Internet Use Among Older Homeless Adults", *JMIR mHealth uHealth*, 2018. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30530464 | 300 homeless adults aged 50+, Oakland (2013–14) | 72.3% had a phone; 32.1% of those a smartphone; of those who ever had a phone, 53.1% had one stolen and 52.9% had lost one |
| Galperin, Bar & Nguyen, "The power divide: Mobile communication in Los Angeles' Skid Row", *Mobile Media & Communication*, 2020. doi:10.1177/2050157920932608 | 106 adults, Skid Row (2018) | 76.4% owned a phone; over half changed number in the past six months (theft, loss, street sweeps); ~60% found charging difficult |

**What this supports.** Many participants will have a phone at any moment, but numbers and devices
change often. Phone contact is useful as one method; it cannot be the foundation (decision D2).

## Asheville service landscape

* **Point-in-Time counts, Asheville–Buncombe CoC (NC-501).** 2025: 755 people, 328 unsheltered
  (unsheltered up 50% from 2024; 116 unsheltered people, 35%, named Helene as the cause; HUD's figure
  additionally counted people in FEMA hotel sheltering). — City of Asheville, 2025-03-28,
  https://www.ashevillenc.gov/news/continuum-of-care-coc-releases-2025-point-in-time-count-numbers/ ·
  2026: 824 people, 334 unsheltered, as reported by Blue Ridge Public Radio (2026-08-31) and
  828newsNOW (2026-04-09); *uncertain:* official 2026 breakdown not found.
* **CoC lead and HMIS.** The City of Asheville's Homeless Strategy Division is the CoC lead agency and
  manages the CoC's HMIS; coordinated entry operates. —
  https://www.ashevillenc.gov/projects/asheville-buncombe-continuum-of-care/
* **Organizations providing outreach, day services or shelter** (status from each organization's
  site or local news, late 2026; none contacted):
  * Homeward Bound of WNC — AHOPE Day Center and street outreach (https://www.homewardboundwnc.org/).
  * BeLoved Asheville — street outreach with a medic team of people with lived experience
    (https://www.belovedasheville.com/our-work/street-outreach/).
  * Haywood Street Congregation — Downtown Welcome Table and Respite (https://www.haywoodstreet.org/).
  * Western Carolina Rescue Ministries — shelters and meals (https://www.westerncarolinarescue.org/).
  * ABCCM — Transformation Village; its Veterans Restoration Quarters was destroyed by Helene and is
    being rebuilt (WLOS, 2026-08-11).
  * Sunrise Community for Recovery and Wellness — drop-in recovery center and peer support
    (https://www.sunriseinasheville.org/); see [legal-review §5](legal-review.md#5-42-cfr-part-2-substance-use-treatment-records--blocking-to-confirm).
  * Helpmate — domestic-violence services and shelter; a referral resource, not a pilot host
    ([legal-review §7](legal-review.md#7-vawa-confidentiality--blocking)).
  * Salvation Army Center of Hope — its 65-bed residential shelter program is paused November
    2026–April 2027 for funding (WLOS, 2026-09-16).
  * Code Purple 2026–27 — cold-weather shelter 1 November–30 April at 32°F or below (or 33–40°F with
    precipitation), no sobriety or ID required; sites listed as Homeward Bound at the Center of Hope
    and ABCCM Transformation Village (City CoC page above). *Uncertain:* the Center of Hope building
    appears to host Code Purple while the Salvation Army's own program is paused; confirm with the
    organizations.

**What this supports.** Asheville has several organizations with daily contact, a by-name list, and
a strained shelter system; people move among organizations, which is why relays (Stage B) matter.

## Hurricane Helene

* **Deaths.** 107 verified storm-related deaths in North Carolina, 42 in Buncombe County, as of
  2025-06-17. The state's table lists county and count only, with no housing status. — NCDHHS,
  https://www.ncdhhs.gov/assistance/disaster-preparation-and-recovery/hurricane-helene-recovery-resources/hurricane-helene-storm-related-fatalities
* **Deaths among people experiencing homelessness.** No official or credible count was found.
* **The informal "more than 250" figure.** No traceable source was found. *Unconfirmed
  possibility:* Helene's total across all affected states was reported in early October 2024 as
  exceeding 250, which may be the origin of the figure. It must not be cited as a count of homeless
  deaths.
* **Why such counts are hard.** There is no official national count of deaths among people
  experiencing homelessness and housing status is rarely recorded on death records. — National
  Health Care for the Homeless Council, *Homeless Mortality Data Toolkit*, 2021,
  https://nhchc.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Homeless-Mortality-Toolkit-FULL-FINAL.pdf
* **Disruption relevant to continuity planning.**
  * Water: Asheville's boil-water notice was lifted on 2024-11-18 after a 53-day water crisis
    (BPR, 2024-11-18); about 90–95% of the system had non-potable water back by 2024-10-18 (Asheville
    Watchdog via NC Health News).
  * Power: roughly 43% of Buncombe customers were still without power around 2024-10-07 (NC Medical
    Society citing poweroutage.us).
  * Mobile: 285 of 348 Buncombe cell sites (81.9%) were out on 2024-09-28 and 41 (11.8%) on
    2024-10-14. — FCC Communications Status Reports,
    https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-405893A1.txt and
    https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-406529A1.txt

**What this supports.** For weeks, phones, internet and the host's building may all be unavailable
at once, and staff may themselves be displaced — the basis for off-site hosting, paper fallback and
roll call in [07](07-disaster-continuity.md).

## Disaster reunification tools

The American Red Cross's former "Safe and Well" site now redirects to a "Contact and locate loved
ones" page (*inference:* the old site appears retired; no announcement found). Active help covers an
Emergency Welfare Inquiry for people who are elderly, have functional or access needs, have a
medical or mental-health condition worsened by the disaster, or have difficulty with English; family
reunification for people who lived together; and military welfare inquiries. —
https://www.redcross.org/get-help/disaster-relief-and-recovery-services/contact-and-locate-loved-ones.html
